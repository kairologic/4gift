<!-- visual: callout | Short list: 4 kinds of teen and a gift for each, plus 1 skip -->

For a teen's birthday, Christmas or graduation, the gifts that land are the ones they'll carry, show off or use with friends. Here's the short list:

- **For the teen who's always on the move:** an insulated bottle. Owala says the FreeSip seals with a leak-proof lid and keeps drinks cold for 24 hours.
- **For the social teen:** an instant camera. Fujifilm says the Instax Mini 12 sets its own exposure and has a selfie mirror, and prints develop in about 90 seconds.
- **For the reader:** an e-reader, or a bookstore gift card if they love paper.
- **For the gamer or music fan:** wireless earbuds, a gaming headset or an LED light strip for their room.
- **Skip** anything that tries too hard to be cool, and clothing in a size or style you're guessing at.

## How do you pick a gift a teen will actually use?

<!-- visual: table | 6 habits matched to a gift, why it lands and skip it if, from 4 places to look -->

Look at what they already carry and do with friends. Teens use gifts that fit into their day, not ones that ask them to start a new hobby.

Their phone, bag, room and friends are the four places to look. If you can picture the gift in one of those, it's a good bet.

| If they... | Give them | Why it lands | Skip it if |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carry a backpack | Insulated bottle that seals | Used every school day | They already have one they love |
| Love hanging out | Instant camera | Prints to share on the spot | They only want phone photos |
| Read a lot | E-reader or bookstore card | A library in the bag | They love paper books |
| Game or stream | Gaming headset | Clear chat, quieter house | Their setup already has one |
| Decorate their room | LED light strip | Changes the room from a phone | Rental rules on adhesive |
| Travel or commute | Portable power bank | Keeps the phone alive | They're never far from a plug |

## Which water bottle do teens want?

<!-- visual: callout | Owala FreeSip: leak-proof lid, 24 hours cold, 24 to 40 oz -->

The one that seals in a backpack. Owala calls the FreeSip lid leak-proof, and it comes in 24, 32 and 40 ounce sizes.

The FreeSip also lets them sip through a built-in straw or tilt and swig. The Stanley Quencher is popular too, but REI describes its lid as made to resist splashes, not as leakproof, so it's a car and desk bottle more than a bag one. One more note for the gift card: Owala says to hand-wash the steel cup, though the lid can go on the dishwasher's top rack. Our [water bottle comparison](/guides/owala-freesip-vs-stanley-quencher-vs-yeti-rambler/) has all three side by side.

## Is an instant camera a good gift for a teen?

<!-- visual: callout | Instax Mini 12: prints in about 90 seconds, close-up at 12 to 19 in -->

Yes, for a teen who loves being with friends. It makes a photo they can hold, trade and stick on a wall, which a phone can't.

Fujifilm says the Instax Mini 12 adjusts shutter speed and flash to the light on its own, so there are no settings to learn. It has a selfie mirror and a close-up mode for 12 to 19 inches, and prints develop in about 90 seconds. Add a few packs of film. A camera with no film is a disappointing first hour.

## What about the teen who reads?

An e-reader if they read a lot, especially if they borrow from the library. The Kobo Clara BW has Libby built in, says Today.com, and myereader notes it has no ads by default.

The Kindle Paperwhite suits a teen who reads for school as well as fun. Myereader says Kindles are better study tools, and Amazon claims up to 12 weeks on a charge at half an hour a day, as iFixit reports. Both are rated IPX8, good for 2 meters of fresh water for 60 minutes. Our [e-reader comparison](/guides/kindle-paperwhite-vs-kobo-clara-bw/) explains the trade-offs.

## What's a good graduation gift for a teen?

Something for the next chapter: a portable power bank, packing cubes, a slim wallet or a leather-bound journal. They're useful at college, at a first job or on a gap year.

An item tracker for keys and bags is a practical pick for a teen heading out on their own. A bookstore gift card works for the reader who's about to have a lot more free time, or a lot more reading lists.

## What should you avoid giving a teen?

<!-- visual: callout | 4 kinds of teen gifts to skip -->

Gifts that guess at their taste. Teens' tastes change fast, and a miss is hard to hide.

- **Clothing in a guessed size or style:** a gift card to the store they already shop at is safer.
- **"Educational" gifts they didn't ask for:** a study planner says homework, not birthday.
- **Toys for a younger age:** check that it doesn't feel like a gift for their little sibling.
- **Anything that needs your account:** if it uses an app, let them set it up with their own login.

## So what would I give?

An insulated bottle that seals for most teens, and an instant camera for the one who's always out with friends. Both get used right away, and neither needs explaining.

For a reader, an e-reader with a gift card for the first few books. When in doubt, a gift card to the place they already love beats a guess every time.

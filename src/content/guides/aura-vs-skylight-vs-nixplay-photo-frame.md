For a grandparent, a mom or anyone who lives far from the family, a digital photo frame is a gift that keeps getting new photos long after the holiday. The three big names differ most in how photos get onto the frame and what's free:

- **Aura** for the family that lives on their phones. Aura says photos go to the frame from its app, with no subscription and unlimited photos and videos.
- **Skylight** for the recipient who doesn't use apps. Family emails photos to the frame's own address, and the Skylight Frame 2 is a touchscreen.
- **Nixplay** for the family that wants to share across several frames. Nixplay takes photos by app, web or email, and its free plan connects up to 5 frames.
- **Skip a frame** for a home without reliable Wi-Fi. Every one of these needs Wi-Fi to receive new photos.

## Who will send the photos, and how?

<!-- visual: table | Best for, photo sending, screens from 10 to 12 in, free storage and paid plans for all 3 -->

That's the real question, because the recipient rarely loads the frame. The family does.

Aura is built around its app. NBC Select's reviewer says everything, from uploading photos to inviting family, runs through the Aura app, and notes that Aura has an email option too. If the whole family is on smartphones, this is the smoothest.

Skylight leans on email. PhotoWorkout says family can email photos to a dedicated address that lands on the frame, with no app needed on the sender's end. That's the easiest path for a cousin who'll never install another app.

Nixplay does both. Its site lists mobile, web app and email photo upload.

## What's free, and what costs extra later?

<!-- visual: table | Free vs paid: Nixplay free 500 MB and 15 s clips vs Plus unlimited and 2 min clips -->

Aura is the simplest answer. Its site says there are no subscription fees and unlimited photos and videos, and NBC Select lists Aura's cloud as unlimited and free.

Skylight and Nixplay keep some extras behind a paid tier. PhotoWorkout says Skylight's core photo display is free, while extras like the calendar and longer videos are in Skylight Plus. Nixplay's site lists its free plan as 500 MB of photo cloud storage, 15-second video clips and 1 shared album, with Nixplay Plus adding unlimited photo storage and 2-minute clips.

We left out what the plans cost. Check each plan page before you give, so the recipient isn't surprised by an upgrade prompt.

## Which frame is easiest for someone who isn't techy?

Skylight. PhotoWorkout calls it the easiest for less tech-savvy users, because family can email photos in and the frame is a touchscreen the recipient can tap.

Skylight also says existing photos keep showing with or without Wi-Fi, and that Wi-Fi is only needed for new photos and features. That helps in a home where the internet drops now and then.

Aura works with touch bars on the sides of the frame, according to NBC Select, rather than a touchscreen. Some people find that simpler. Others go looking for a screen to tap.

## Which one shows photos best?

NBC Select gave picture quality to Aura in its head-to-head of the Aura Aspen and the Skylight Frame 2, and preferred the Aura frame overall.

Screen shape differs too. NBC Select lists the Aspen at 12 inches with a 4:3 screen, and the Skylight Frame 2 at 10 inches with a wider 16:9 screen. Think about the photos the family takes most, and pick the shape that crops them least.

## How many people can share one frame?

<!-- visual: chart | Frames linked: Nixplay free 5, Nixplay Plus 10 -->

All three let several people send to one frame. The difference is how many frames and albums you can link.

- **Aura:** NBC Select says the app lets you invite friends and family to share photos.
- **Skylight:** anyone with the frame's email address can send photos.
- **Nixplay:** the free plan connects up to 5 frames with 1 shared album, and Nixplay Plus raises that to 10 frames and unlimited shared albums, per Nixplay's site.

For a gift to both grandmas, Nixplay's frame linking is the reason to look at it.

## What should you set up before you wrap it?

<!-- visual: callout | 4 setup steps before wrapping, starting with 20 to 30 photos -->

A little, so the frame works the first time it's plugged in.

1. Decide who manages it. One person in the family should own the account.
2. Load 20 or 30 photos first, so the screen isn't empty on day one.
3. Write the frame's email address, or the app name, on the gift card for the rest of the family.
4. Check the recipient's Wi-Fi name and password. They'll need both at setup.

## So which frame would I give?

The Aura for most families, and the Skylight for a recipient who'll never touch an app.

NBC Select preferred the Aura, and its free unlimited storage means no plan to think about later. Skylight's email address and touchscreen make it the gentler gift for a grandparent. Nixplay is the one to pick when the photos need to reach several frames at once.

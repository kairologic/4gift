<!-- visual: callout | Short list: 3 groups of gifts for new parents, plus 1 thing to skip -->

For new parents, whether it's a baby shower, a first visit or the holidays after a birth, the best gifts look after the parents as much as the baby. Here's the short list:

- **For the parents:** a heated mug for the coffee that keeps going cold, an insulated bottle that seals, or a gift card for meals.
- **For sharing the baby:** a Wi-Fi photo frame for the grandparents, so new photos reach them without a single text.
- **For the nursery:** a book light for night feeds, a custom photo book of the first year, or a handprint keepsake kit.
- **Skip** blankets, pillows, bumpers, stuffed toys and anything weighted for the crib. The AAP says none of these belong in a baby's sleep space.

## What do new parents actually need?

<!-- visual: table | 6 gifts with who it's for, why it helps and skip it if -->

Time, food and a hot drink. Most baby gear arrives from a registry, so the gift that stands out is often the one for the parents.

Think about what the first few weeks look like: short nights, long feeds and coffee left on the counter. A gift that fixes one of those small daily annoyances gets used every day.

| Gift | Who it's for | Why it helps | Skip it if |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heated mug | The coffee or tea drinker | Holds a set temperature | They only drink cold drinks |
| Insulated bottle | The parent who's always on the go | Seals for the diaper bag | They have a favorite already |
| Meal gift card | Both parents | One less thing to plan | You can bring food yourself |
| Wi-Fi photo frame | Grandparents | Photos arrive without texts | Grandparents lack Wi-Fi |
| Book light | The night-feed parent | Reading light at 3 a.m. | They read on a phone |
| Photo book | The whole family | Keeps the first year in print | They'd rather pick photos themselves |

## Which gift helps with cold coffee?

<!-- visual: chart | Ember Mug 2: 120 to 145°F, up to 1.5 hours off its coaster -->

A heated mug. Best Buy says the Ember Mug 2 holds a temperature you set, from 120 to 145°F, for up to 1.5 hours off its coaster and all day on it.

For a parent who wants no app, AOL's reviewer describes the Nextmug as working from one button with three presets, and Bob Vila lists it at 14 ounces. The Ember is hand wash only, per idrinkcoffee, so it's a gift for someone who doesn't mind a quick rinse. Our [heated mug comparison](/guides/ember-mug-2-vs-nextmug-vs-ohom-ui/) has the details.

## What's a good gift for the grandparents?

A Wi-Fi photo frame that the parents can fill from their phones. It's really a gift to both sides of the family.

PhotoWorkout says Skylight lets family email photos to the frame's own address, with no app needed by the sender. Aura runs on its app, and its site says there's no subscription and unlimited photos and videos. NBC Select preferred the Aura overall, and PhotoWorkout called Skylight the easiest for less tech-savvy users. Load 20 or 30 photos before you wrap it, so the frame is full when it's opened.

## What baby gifts should you avoid?

<!-- visual: callout | Safe sleep check: 3 things to keep out of the crib, no incline over 10 degrees -->

Anything that goes in the crib except a fitted sheet. CPSC calls it "Bare is Best": nothing but a fitted sheet in a crib, bassinet or play yard.

> **Before you give a sleep gift,** check it against the safe sleep advice:
> - No blankets, pillows, stuffed toys or bumper pads in the baby's bed, says the AAP.
> - No weighted blankets, sleepers or swaddles on or near the baby, say the AAP and CPSC.
> - No inclined sleepers. The AAP and CPSC say a surface that inclines more than 10 degrees isn't safe for sleep.
>
> NICHD's Safe to Sleep campaign says a baby's sleep surface should be firm, flat and level, and covered only with a fitted sheet.

So a plush toy is fine for play, but it's a gift for the shelf, not the crib. If you want to give bedding, a pack of fitted crib sheets in the right size is the safe version.

## Is secondhand baby gear a good gift?

Some of it, with care. Hand-me-down clothes and books are lovely. Cribs and car seats need checking first.

The AAP says to check the CPSC website to make sure a crib hasn't been recalled, especially if it's not new, and not to use a crib that's missing hardware, instructions or parts. CPSC points parents to SaferProducts.gov for recalls. For car seats, the AAP's car seat guide says a seat is for travel only, not for sleeping, and it suggests working with a certified child passenger safety technician before the baby is born. A used car seat with an unknown history is a gift to skip.

## What can you give that isn't a thing?

<!-- visual: callout | 4 kinds of help to promise on a card, with a real date -->

Help. A meal train, a delivered dinner, a few hours of babysitting for an older sibling, or a load of laundry done can mean more than anything wrapped.

Put it on a card with a real date. "I'll bring dinner on the 14th" gets used. "Let me know if you need anything" usually doesn't.

## So what would I give?

A heated mug for the parent who loves coffee, and a photo frame for the grandparents. Both get used every day, and neither adds to the pile of baby gear.

Pair either one with a meal or a promised afternoon of help. And if you're tempted by something soft for the crib, keep it for playtime: the AAP, CPSC and NICHD all say the sleep space stays bare.

<!-- visual: callout | Short list: 3 kinds of gift that create contact, plus 1 to skip -->

For a grandparent, whether across town or across the country, the gift that does the most is the one that brings the family back every week. Here's the short list:

- **A window into the week:** a Wi-Fi photo frame the whole family can send photos to from their phones.
- **Something to do together:** a custom jigsaw puzzle made from a family photo, worked on over a visit or two.
- **A standing date:** a weekly call at a set time, a monthly lunch, or a class you sign up for together.
- **Skip** gifts that sit on a shelf after the holiday. They are fine, but they don't do the job this piece is about.

## How lonely are older adults, really?

<!-- visual: chart | Felt lonely, adults 50 to 80: 34% in 2018, up to 42% in the pandemic, 33% in 2024 -->

About one in three. In 2024, 33% of adults 50 to 80 said they felt lonely some of the time or often, according to the University of Michigan's National Poll on Healthy Aging, published in JAMA in December 2024. That is about the same as in 2018, when it was 34%.

The pandemic pushed it higher, to as many as 42%, and it has come back down. But lead researcher Dr. Preeti Malani put the catch plainly: that 2018 baseline "was not good." The same poll found 29% felt isolated some or much of the time in 2024, against 27% in 2018.

Health matters a lot here. Among people who rated their physical health fair or poor, 53% reported loneliness in 2024. For a grandparent who has slowed down or stopped driving, a visit is harder to arrange, which is exactly when regular contact counts most.

## Is it only the oldest grandparents?

No. The poll found adults 50 to 64 reported more loneliness and isolation than those 65 to 80, in every year studied, and their rates had not returned to pre-pandemic levels in 2024.

So the young grandparent who is still working is not automatically in the clear. Neither is the one who looks busy.

## How often do older adults see people?

<!-- visual: chart | Social contact once a week or less: 28% in 2018, 46% in June 2020, 33% in January 2023 -->

Less often than you might think. In the poll's January 2023 survey, 33% of adults 50 to 80 said they had social contact with family they don't live with once a week or less. In 2018 it was 28%, and in June 2020 it reached 46%.

That number points to what a gift can actually change: frequency. A present opened once in December doesn't move it. Something that creates a reason to talk every week can.

## Which gifts create regular contact?

<!-- visual: table | 3 gifts with how it creates contact, how often, and skip it if -->

Gifts that need the family to keep showing up, in person or on a screen.

| Gift | How it creates contact | How often | Skip it if |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wi-Fi photo frame | Family send photos from their phones | Whenever anyone shares | No Wi-Fi at their place |
| Custom photo puzzle | A project to do together on a visit | Each visit until it's done | Hands or eyesight make small pieces hard |
| A standing date | A call, lunch or class on the calendar | Weekly or monthly | They'd rather keep it spontaneous |

The **Wi-Fi photo frame** is the strongest of the three, because it keeps working after the gift is opened. Grandchildren's soccer games and school plays arrive as they happen, and each new photo is a reason to call. Set it up before you wrap it, add the family's emails or phones, and load it with 20 or 30 photos so it's full on day one. Our [photo frame comparison](/guides/aura-vs-skylight-vs-nixplay-photo-frame/) covers which frames let a sender skip the app.

A **custom photo puzzle** made from a family picture is slower, and that is the point. It is a reason to sit at the same table for an afternoon, and then another.

## What if the gift is your time?

Then put it on the calendar, not on a card that says "anytime."

The University of Michigan poll director, Dr. Jeffrey Kullgren, pointed people toward community resources like senior centers, veterans' groups and volunteering. A family can borrow that idea on a small scale: something with a fixed time, which happens whether or not anyone remembers to suggest it.

Some versions that work:

- **The Sunday call,** same time every week, with a grandchild who takes a turn.
- **A monthly lunch,** booked for the year, written into the card with the dates.
- **A class for two,** like pottery or a museum lecture series, with a grandparent and one grandchild going together.
- **A letter exchange** with a younger grandchild, with stamped, addressed envelopes in the gift box so replies are easy.

The gift is the promise to keep doing it. If it lapses in February, it was a nice idea, not a gift.

## So what would I give?

I'd give a Wi-Fi photo frame, already filled with photos and already connected to every phone in the family. It keeps bringing the family into the room long after the holiday is over.

Then I'd add one standing date to the card, with a real day and time. A third of adults 50 to 80 say they feel lonely, and more than half of those in fair or poor physical health do. A gift that makes contact routine is the one that answers that.

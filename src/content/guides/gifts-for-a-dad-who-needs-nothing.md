<!-- visual: callout | 4 ways to find a gift for a dad who needs nothing, plus 1 skip -->

For the dad who says he needs nothing, at Christmas, a birthday or Father's Day, the best gift is one he'd never buy himself but uses every day. Here's how to find it:

- **Upgrade something he already uses:** a heated mug for the coffee he forgets, or an insulated bottle that keeps drinks cold for 24 hours, as Owala says the FreeSip does.
- **Give him something to do:** a 1000-piece jigsaw puzzle, a strategy board game or a plan for a day out together.
- **Give him something to remember:** a custom photo book, or a Wi-Fi photo frame the whole family sends photos to.
- **Give him something he'll use up:** a coffee bean sampler or a gourmet snack box. Nothing to store, nothing to dust.
- **Skip** gadgets he has to learn, set up or subscribe to, unless you'll set it up for him.

## Why is "nothing" the hardest gift?

<!-- visual: table | 6 everyday habits matched to a gift, why it lands and skip it if -->

Because it usually means "nothing I can think of," not "nothing at all." Dads who say it tend to own what they need and replace it only when it breaks.

So the trick is to look at what he does every day and make one part of it better. Watch a normal weekend. What does he drink, carry, fix, read or complain about? That's the list.

| If he... | Give him | Why it lands | Skip it if |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reheats his coffee | A heated mug or mug warmer | Ends the microwave trips | He drinks it in one go |
| Carries a water bottle | An insulated bottle that seals | Gets used every day | His old one still works |
| Grills on weekends | A wireless meat thermometer | Takes out the guesswork | He already has one |
| Reads every night | An e-reader | Holds a whole library | He loves paper books |
| Misses the grandkids | A Wi-Fi photo frame | New photos without texts | No Wi-Fi at home |
| Has every gadget | A coffee or snack sampler | Nothing to store | Dietary limits |

## What's a gift he'll use every day?

<!-- visual: chart | Heated mugs: Ember 120 to 145°F and 1.5 hours, OHOM Ui 130°F on its pad -->

The coffee mug he already reaches for, made better. Best Buy says the Ember Mug 2 holds a temperature you set, from 120 to 145°F, for up to 1.5 hours away from its coaster.

If he hates apps, the Nextmug has no Bluetooth and no app, per AOL's reviewer, just a button with three presets. For the dad who sits at one desk, Bob Vila says the OHOM Ui mug warms to 130°F on its pad, with no battery to charge. Our [heated mug comparison](/guides/ember-mug-2-vs-nextmug-vs-ohom-ui/) goes through all three.

## What about the dad who reads?

<!-- visual: callout | E-readers: up to 12 weeks claimed battery, IPX8 for 60 minutes -->

An e-reader, if he reads a lot and doesn't mind a screen. Amazon claims the Kindle Paperwhite lasts up to 12 weeks on a charge at half an hour a day, as iFixit reports.

If he borrows from the library, the Kobo Clara BW has Libby built in, according to Today.com. Both are rated IPX8 for 2 meters of fresh water for 60 minutes, per comparisontabl.es, so a book by the pool is fine. Add a bookstore gift card so the first few books are on you. Our [e-reader comparison](/guides/kindle-paperwhite-vs-kobo-clara-bw/) covers which one suits whom.

## What if he really has everything?

Give him time, not a thing. A puzzle to do together, a board game night, tickets to a game, or a day out on the water count as gifts, and they don't add to the garage.

Consumables are the other safe bet. A coffee bean sampler, a gourmet snack box or a whiskey glasses set he'll use with what he already drinks all work. They're easy to give and easy to enjoy, and nothing has to find a shelf.

## Is a sentimental gift too much?

Not for most dads. A custom photo book of the year, or a Wi-Fi photo frame the family keeps filling, often gets the biggest reaction.

A frame keeps giving after the day itself. PhotoWorkout says Skylight lets the family email photos to the frame's own address, with no app needed. Aura's site says its frames have no subscription and unlimited photos and videos. Load 20 or 30 photos first, so it's full when he opens it, and see our [photo frame comparison](/guides/aura-vs-skylight-vs-nixplay-photo-frame/) for which one fits.

## What should you avoid giving?

<!-- visual: callout | 4 kinds of gifts that turn into chores -->

Anything that turns into a chore. A dad who says he needs nothing usually means he doesn't want another thing to manage.

- **Setup-heavy gadgets:** if it needs an account, an app and a firmware update, set it up before you wrap it.
- **Subscriptions he didn't ask for:** a monthly charge feels like a bill, not a gift.
- **Novelty items:** a joke gift gets a laugh and then a drawer.
- **Duplicates:** check that his old one is actually worn out before you replace it.

## So what would I give?

A heated mug if he drinks coffee, or a photo frame if he has grandkids. Both fit into a day he already has, and both get used without a second thought.

If you're still stuck, give him an afternoon. Put the plan on a card, with a date. It's the one gift a dad who needs nothing can't already own.

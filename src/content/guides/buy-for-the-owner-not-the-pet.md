<!-- visual: callout | Short list: 3 gifts for the pet owner, plus 1 to skip -->

For a friend, sister or coworker whose dog or cat is family, the gift they remember is usually the one for them, not the animal. Here's the short list:

- **A portrait:** their pet, painted or illustrated from a photo by an artist who works to order.
- **A keepsake:** something made in their pet's likeness that lasts longer than the pet will.
- **Help:** a dated promise of pet sitting, dog walking or a ride to the vet.
- **Skip** the generic toy-and-treat box unless it's a small add-on. The pet will enjoy it; the owner won't remember it.

## How much do owners think of their pets?

<!-- visual: chart | Pew 2023, US pet owners: 97% say pets are family, 51% say as much as a human member -->

As family, almost without exception. In a Pew Research Center survey of 5,073 US adults, including 2,963 pet owners, taken April 10 to 16, 2023, 97% of pet owners said their pets are part of their family.

The sharper number is the next one. About half, 51%, said their pets are as much a part of their family as a human member. Women were more likely to say so than men (57% to 43%), and dog-only owners slightly more than cat-only owners (53% to 48%).

That changes the gift. If the pet is as much family as a person, a gift that honors the pet is personal to the owner in the way a family photo is.

## How many people does this cover?

<!-- visual: callout | APPA 2025: 95 million US households with a pet, 71 million with a dog, 53 million with a cat -->

A lot. The American Pet Products Association says 95 million US households owned at least one pet in 2025. Dog ownership rose from 51% of US households in 2024 to 53% in 2025, or 71 million households, and cats were in 39% of households, or 53 million.

Pew's figure lines up: 62% of Americans own a pet, and 35% have more than one. Odds are good that several people on your list count a pet as family.

## Why does the treat box miss?

Because the owner already buys the pet what it needs, and they are being careful about it. APPA reports that 22% of pet owners spent less on their pets in 2025, and that spending is shifting away from discretionary items toward essential care. APPA's president, Pete Scott, put it as consumers "becoming more intentional" with their spending.

So a box of chews is a small treat the owner has probably decided they can do without. It isn't wrong. It just isn't the gift that says you understand what the animal means to them.

## What makes a pet gift memorable for the owner?

<!-- visual: table | 3 gifts with what it says, made how, and skip it if -->

It is made for them, about the pet, and it lasts.

| Gift | What it says | Made how | Skip it if |
|---|---|---|---|
| Custom pet portrait | Your pet matters to me too | Painted or drawn to order from a photo | You can't get a good photo of the pet |
| Custom plush likeness | A copy of them to keep | Sewn to order from photos | They'd find it uncanny |
| Pet-sitting days | I'll look after them for you | Your time, on a set date | The pet only trusts the owner |

A **custom pet portrait** is the classic for good reason. A small-studio artist works from the owner's own photo, so it is one of a kind, and it hangs on the wall for years. Get the photo quietly: a clear, well-lit shot with the pet's face in focus gives the artist the most to work with. Ask a partner or roommate if you can't ask the owner without spoiling it.

A **custom plush made in the pet's likeness** is the newer kind of keepsake. Makers sew it to order from several photos, matching markings and coloring. It is a stranger gift than a portrait, so it suits the owner with a sense of humor, or a child who loves the family dog.

## What does "help on the hard days" look like?

<!-- visual: callout | 4 kinds of help to promise on a card, with a real date -->

A promise with a date. Pet owners can't easily travel, get sick or work late without a plan for the animal, and a friend who covers that is giving something a store can't.

Versions that get used:

- **A weekend of pet sitting,** so they can finally take the trip they keep putting off.
- **Dog walks during a busy week,** a move, a new baby or a stretch of late nights.
- **A ride to the vet** for the owner who doesn't drive, or for the cat who has to go in a carrier on a bus.
- **Company at a hard appointment,** for an older pet's checkup when the news may not be good.

Write the day on the card. "I'll take Biscuit the weekend of the 21st" gets used. "Happy to dog-sit sometime" usually doesn't.

## So what would I give?

For most pet lovers, I'd give a custom portrait of their pet, painted from a photo I'd quietly gotten from someone close to them. It's personal, it's made to order, and it stays on the wall long after a toy is chewed through.

For a friend who never gets away, I'd give a weekend of pet sitting with a real date on the card. Pew found half of owners see their pet as much family as any person. Looking after that family member is a gift in its own right.

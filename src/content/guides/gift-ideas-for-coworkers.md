For a coworker, a boss or the whole team at the holidays, the best gift is small, useful at a desk and safe for someone you don't know well. Here's the short list, sorted by how well you know them:

- **Barely know them:** a scented soy candle, a loose-leaf tea sampler or a gourmet snack box. Pleasant, consumable, no guessing about taste.
- **Sit near them:** a mug warmer or a heated mug. HGTV named the OHOM Ui Plus its best self-heating mug for the office.
- **Know their habits:** an insulated water bottle for the gym-goer, or a rechargeable book light for the lunch-break reader.
- **Whole team:** a low-maintenance desk plant for each desk, or one big snack box for the break room.
- **Skip** anything personal, like clothing, perfume or jewelry, and anything with a joke that only half the office will get.

## What makes a good coworker gift?

<!-- visual: table | 6 gifts with best for, why it works and skip it if, after 3 quick checks -->

One that's useful at work and easy to say yes to. If they'd be fine opening it in front of the team, it passes.

Three quick checks help:

1. **It fits on a desk.** Small enough to live next to a keyboard or go home in a bag.
2. **It isn't personal.** No sizes, no scents worn on the body, nothing about looks.
3. **It's easy to use.** No setup, no subscription, no app they didn't ask for.

| Gift | Best for | Why it works | Skip it if |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heated mug or mug warmer | Desk coffee drinkers | Ends the cold coffee problem | They work standing or roaming |
| Insulated bottle | Gym-goers, water sippers | Gets carried every day | They already carry one |
| Tea sampler | Tea drinkers, anyone unknown | Consumable, easy to share | They only drink coffee |
| Snack box | Teams, break rooms | Everyone can take a share | Allergies in the office |
| Desk plant | New hires, quiet desks | Low upkeep, lasts all year | No light at their desk |
| Book light | Readers | Small, useful at home too | They read on a tablet |

## Which gift works for a coffee drinker at a desk?

<!-- visual: chart | Mug heat: OHOM Ui 130°F on its pad, Ember 120 to 145°F and 1.5 hours off its coaster -->

A heated mug that stays on its pad. HGTV picked the OHOM Ui Plus as its office choice, and Bob Vila says the Ui mug heats to 130°F with no battery in the mug.

That means no app to pair and no charging to remember. Coolmomtech's reviewer notes the catch: it only works on its pad, so you can't walk around the office with it. For the coworker who moves between meetings, the Ember Mug 2 holds temperature for up to 1.5 hours off its coaster, per Best Buy, and sets anywhere from 120 to 145°F in its app.

A plain mug warmer is the simplest version of the same idea. It keeps a cup warm on the desk, with no electronics in the mug at all. Bob Vila warns that most warmers aren't strong enough to reheat a drink that has gone fully cold, so it's for keeping coffee warm, not rescuing it.

## What about the coworker who goes to the gym?

<!-- visual: callout | Owala FreeSip: 24 hours cold, 24, 32 or 40 oz -->

An insulated bottle they can throw in a bag. Owala calls the FreeSip lid leak-proof and says the bottle keeps drinks cold for 24 hours, in 24, 32 or 40 ounce sizes.

If they drive in, the Stanley Quencher H2.0 is the commuter's pick. REI says the 40 oz Quencher fits most car cup holders and is dishwasher safe. It resists splashes rather than being leakproof, so it's a desk and car bottle, not a bag bottle.

## What's a safe gift for someone you barely know?

Something they'll use up. A candle, a tea sampler, a coffee bean sampler or a box of chocolates all land well, and none of them clutters a desk for a year.

Two notes keep these safe:

- **Snacks:** check for allergies first, or choose a box that lists its ingredients clearly. In a shared office, a nut-free option is the kindest default.
- **Candles:** some offices don't allow open flames. A candle is a take-home gift, so say so on the card.

## What should you give the whole team?

One shared gift, or one small, identical gift for every desk. Identical matters: nobody compares.

A big snack box for the break room is the easiest. A row of low-maintenance desk plants works for a smaller team, and a plant that copes with low light suits an office with few windows. For a remote team, mail-friendly gifts like tea samplers or a bookstore gift card skip the shipping headaches.

## Are there gifts to avoid at work?

<!-- visual: callout | 4 kinds of gifts to skip at work -->

Yes. Skip anything that could make the moment awkward in front of colleagues.

- **Personal items:** clothing, perfume, lotions and jewelry suggest you've thought about their body or looks.
- **Alcohol:** not everyone drinks, and some workplaces don't allow it.
- **Jokes:** a gag gift about age, weight or work habits can sting more than it amuses.
- **Big spends:** a gift that clearly outweighs what others gave can feel like pressure. Keep it in line with the team.

If you're the boss, give down, not up. A gift from a manager to the team is common. A pricey gift from one team member to the boss can look like a favor.

## So what would I give?

For a coworker you sit near, a heated mug or a mug warmer. It solves a daily problem at the desk, and HGTV and Bob Vila both point to the OHOM Ui for an office.

For someone you barely know, a tea sampler or a snack box. For a whole team, one shared snack box or matching desk plants. Add a short handwritten note, which is often the part people keep.

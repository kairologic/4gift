<!-- visual: callout | Short answer: 3 blankets and who each suits, plus who should skip -->

For the friend who's always cold on the couch, the partner who reads in bed, or a mom who loves a nap, a weighted blanket is a cozy gift. The three that come up most are built very differently:

- **Bearaby Napper** for the person who runs warm. It's knit from thick yarn with no beads, and Mattress Clarity calls the Tree Napper incredibly breathable.
- **Gravity Blanket** for the person who's always cold. Mattress Clarity says it doesn't do much for cooling, and Reviewed named it its best weighted blanket.
- **YnM** for someone who needs a specific weight or a bed-size blanket. Mattress Clarity lists 10 weights from 5 to 30 pounds, with some in twin, queen or king.
- **Skip a weighted blanket** for a baby, a toddler, or anyone who can't lift it off by themselves. The safety section below says why.

## Do they run hot or cold?

<!-- visual: table | Fill, weights from 5 to 35 lb, washing and skip it if for all 3 -->

Ask this first, because it's the biggest difference between these three.

Bearaby's Napper has no fill. Its weight comes from the knit itself, and Reviewed says it "definitely isn't going to keep you warm" because the open knit doesn't hold heat. For a hot sleeper, that's the point.

Gravity and YnM use glass beads inside the blanket, per Mattress Clarity. Its review says the Gravity Blanket suits people who get cold quickly and that hot sleepers should look elsewhere. Its roundup lists "might trap heat" as a con for YnM.

| | Bearaby Napper | Gravity Blanket | YnM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best for | Warm sleepers, style fans | Cold sleepers | Picking an exact weight or bed size |
| Fill | None, knit yarn | Glass beads | Glass beads and cotton |
| Weights | 10 to 25 lb (Cotton) | 15, 20, 25 and 35 lb | 5 to 30 lb, 10 options |
| Washing | Whole blanket machine wash | Cover machine wash, inner hand wash | Not stated in our sources |

## What weight should you give?

<!-- visual: chart | 10% rule: 150 lb adult about 15 lb, 200 lb about 20 lb -->

About 10% of the recipient's body weight. Sleep Foundation and Puffy both give that rule of thumb, and Puffy says adults can add 1 or 2 pounds by preference.

So a 150-pound adult would start around 15 pounds. Mattress Clarity says Bearaby's Tree Napper weights suit people roughly 150 to 250 pounds. If you don't know the number, pick the lighter option. Puffy says people with joint pain or breathing or circulation problems should go lighter and talk to a doctor.

## Is a weighted blanket safe for kids?

<!-- visual: callout | Never for babies, not under age 2, plus 3 reasons to check with a doctor -->

Not for babies, ever. Consumer Reports says the American Academy of Pediatrics told the CPSC that weighted sleep products should never be used for babies, and the CPSC now advises caregivers not to use weighted blankets or swaddles for infants.

For children, Sleep Foundation says experts don't usually recommend weighted blankets under age 2, and the child must be able to remove it alone. Somerset NHS goes further for children: it doesn't recommend weighted blankets as therapy, and it says a child must be supervised and the blanket must not be used overnight.

> **Talk to a doctor first if** the recipient:
> - has a condition that affects breathing, body temperature or skin.
> - has joint pain or circulation problems.
> - can't easily push the blanket off on their own.
>
> Somerset NHS and Puffy both list these as reasons to check before using one.

## Which one is easiest to wash?

The Bearaby. Mattress Clarity says the whole Tree Napper is machine washable, cold and alone, tumble dried on low. Reviewed says the same for the Napper.

The Gravity Blanket has a removable cover you can machine wash. The inner beaded blanket is hand wash and air dry, per Mattress Clarity. The roundup we read doesn't give washing instructions for the YnM, so check the label before you give it to someone who'll want to toss it in the machine.

## Which one fits a bed, not just a couch?

<!-- visual: table | Sizes: Bearaby 40 x 64 to 48 x 72 in, Gravity 48 x 72 and 90 x 90 in -->

The YnM. Mattress Clarity says some of its weights come in twin, queen or king sizes.

Bearaby's Nappers are throws. Sleep Foundation lists the Cotton Napper from 40 by 64 inches at 10 pounds up to 48 by 72 at 25 pounds, sized for one person, and Reviewed says even the larger ones aren't big enough to share. Gravity's 15 to 25 pound blankets are 48 by 72 inches, and its 35-pound option is 90 by 90, per Mattress Clarity.

## What if they don't like it?

Check the return terms before you give. Mattress Clarity lists a 30-night trial for Gravity, with free returns minus shipping. Sleep Foundation says Bearaby has a 30-day return policy for items in original, unwashed condition, with a processing fee. Mattress Clarity lists free returns for YnM but no trial.

Include a gift receipt, so the recipient can swap a weight that feels wrong.

## So which one would I give?

The Bearaby Napper for a warm sleeper and the Gravity Blanket for someone who's always cold.

The Bearaby is the stylish, breathable one, and it washes whole. Reviewers split on it, though: Mattress Clarity gave the Tree Napper 5 out of 5 for hot sleepers, while Reviewed's tester didn't think it was worth it and preferred the Gravity. The Gravity is the warm, beaded choice. The YnM is the practical choice when you need an exact weight or a blanket for the whole bed.

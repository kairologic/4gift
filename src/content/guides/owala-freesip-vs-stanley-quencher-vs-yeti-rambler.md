For a teen, a coworker or the friend who's always at the gym, a big insulated bottle is a gift they'll carry every day. The three everyone asks for do different jobs, so match the bottle to the person:

- **Owala FreeSip** for the person who tosses a bottle in a backpack or gym bag. Owala calls the lid leak-proof, and the spout lets you sip through a straw or tilt and swig.
- **Stanley Quencher H2.0 (40 oz)** for the commuter and the desk sipper. It has a handle, a straw, and REI says it fits most car cup holders. It resists splashes but isn't sold as leakproof.
- **YETI Rambler (26 oz) with Chug Cap** for the hiker, the camper and the tradesperson. REI lists it as 100% leakproof, with a sturdy carry handle on the cap.
- **Skip all three** for someone who wants hot coffee on the go. Owala says its steel bottle shouldn't hold hot drinks, and none of these makers lists a hot time on the pages we read.

## Which bottle is it for: bag, car or trail?

<!-- visual: table | Best for, sizes from 24 to 40 oz, leaks, drinking, carry and skip it if for all 3 -->

Start with where it'll ride. That one answer decides most of it.

A bag means it has to seal. Owala calls the FreeSip lid leak-proof, and REI lists the YETI Rambler as 100% leakproof. The Stanley's rotating lid has three positions, a full cover, a drink opening and a straw opening, and REI describes it as made "to resist splashes." That's fine on a desk or in a cup holder, and a gamble on its side in a tote.

A car means it has to fit. REI says the 40 oz Quencher fits most car cup holders. The makers' pages we read don't say the same for the other two, so check the recipient's car if that matters.

A trail means it has to take a knock and carry easily. The YETI's cap is topped with what YETI calls a TripleHaul handle, and REI lists the bottle at 1 lb 4 oz empty.

## How long does each one stay cold?

<!-- visual: chart | Cold time claims: Owala 24 hours, Stanley 11 hours and ice for 2 days, YETI not stated -->

All three are vacuum-insulated stainless steel. The makers state the cold times differently, so read them as claims, not a race.

Owala says the FreeSip stays cold for 24 hours. Stanley says the Quencher stays cold for 11 hours and keeps ice for 2 days. The YETI listing we read doesn't give an hour count.

For most people the difference is a desk day versus a whole weekend in a hot car.

## Which one is easiest to clean?

<!-- visual: callout | Dishwasher: 2 of 3 fully dishwasher safe, Owala lid only -->

The Stanley and the YETI are the easy ones. REI lists both as dishwasher safe.

Owala is more careful. It says the lid is top-rack dishwasher safe and the straw can go in the silverware basket, but it strongly recommends hand-washing the cup itself. For a teen who'll put everything in the dishwasher, that's worth knowing.

The FreeSip's straw lives in the lid, and the Stanley comes with a reusable straw. Straws are one more part to rinse, so a person who hates fiddly cleaning may be happiest with the YETI's simple spout.

## What about the person who drives a lot?

The Stanley Quencher. It's the only one of the three that REI says fits most car cup holders, and the side handle makes it easy to grab from the console.

It's also tall. REI lists it at 10.25 inches without the straw and 12.5 inches with it, so it may not slide under a low cabinet or into a lunch bag. The 40 oz size holds a lot, which is the point for a long commute or a shift.

## Which gift lasts longest?

<!-- visual: chart | REI ratings: YETI 4.7 of 5 from 26,000+ reviews, Stanley 4.3 of 5 from 10,800+ -->

Owala calls its warranty a limited lifetime warranty, and REI says Stanley offers its Built for Life lifetime warranty on the Quencher. We didn't find YETI's warranty terms on the listing we read, so check YETI's own page if that matters to the giver.

Reviewers like all three. REI shows the YETI Rambler at 4.7 out of 5 from more than 26,000 reviews and the Quencher at 4.3 out of 5 from more than 10,800.

## What does each one feel like to drink from?

This is the part people argue about, and it's personal.

- **Owala FreeSip:** you hold it upright and sip through the built-in straw, or tilt it back and drink from the wide opening. Owala's carry loop folds down to lock the lid shut.
- **Stanley Quencher:** a straw through the lid, or rotate the lid to an open sip. It's a cup with a handle more than a bottle.
- **YETI Rambler with Chug Cap:** a wide bottle with a spout made for big gulps. No straw, and the cap comes off for ice.

If you don't know their habit, look at what they carry now. A straw person stays a straw person.

## So which one would I give?

The Owala FreeSip for most people, and the Stanley for anyone who drives.

The FreeSip suits a teen or a gym-goer best. It seals for a bag, it gives a straw and a swig, and it comes in three sizes. The Stanley Quencher also suits the person who sips all day at a desk. The YETI Rambler is the one for the outdoors person who wants a bottle that seals and carries by the cap.

Whichever you choose, add a note about the dishwasher. Owala's hand-wash advice is the one most likely to be missed.
